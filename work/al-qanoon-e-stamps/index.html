The brief

What we were actually solving

Stamp paper in Pakistan has always moved through middlemen. You go to the courts, you find an agent, you pay a fee nobody quotes in advance, and you wait. Al-Qanoon does the same job through the government e-stamping portal and delivers the finished document on WhatsApp, usually within the hour, at any time of day.

That is a genuinely better service. But a better service does not sell itself when the buyer has never heard of you and is about to send you their CNIC number.

The product is invisible

An e-stamp is a government document. Nobody browses for one. People search for it at the moment they need it, often at an hour when no office is open, and they need to trust a stranger with a CNIC number before they have met anyone.

The competition is an agent

The default way to get a stamp paper in the twin cities is a middleman at the courts, with a fee nobody quotes upfront. The site had to make the price and the process more legible than the alternative, not just look better.

Trust before transaction

A first-time visitor has one question: is this legal and is this person real. If the site does not answer that before asking for a CNIC, nothing else matters.

The approach

Six decisions that shaped the site

We did not design a website with a WhatsApp button on it. We designed a WhatsApp conversation with a website in front of it.

01

WhatsApp is the product, not a contact link

In Pakistan the transaction already happens on WhatsApp, so we did not fight it with a form that emails a lead nobody reads. Every path on the site ends in a pre-written WhatsApp message. The site's job is to get someone to press send with confidence, not to capture them.

02

An order builder that writes the message for you

Asking a stressed visitor to compose a message is friction. A three-field builder (purpose, city, stamp value) assembles the WhatsApp text for them. Crucially it sends nothing by itself, and the page says so out loud: you review it in WhatsApp and hit send yourself. Control stays with the visitor, which is the point when the next step is handing over a CNIC.

03

One flat fee, stated before anything is asked

PKR 1,000 appears in the hero, in the pricing block and in the FAQ, with the government stamp duty broken out separately and explicitly never marked up. Publishing the number is what separates the site from the agent at the courts.

04

Legality answered first, not buried in the FAQ

QR-verifiable, issued through the government e-stamping portal, carries a unique ID. That claim is on the page above the fold and expanded in the first FAQ answer, because it is the objection that stops every first order.

05

Built around the 3 AM visitor

The strongest differentiator was that the service runs 24/7/365, so the site repeats it everywhere: the top bar, the hero sub-line, the benefit grid, the FAQ. Someone who needs an affidavit on Eid is the highest-intent visitor there is.

06

Local, specifically

Islamabad and Rawalpindi are named in the title tag, the top bar, the hero chips and the city selector in the order builder. The service is geographically bound and the site never pretends otherwise, which is what makes it findable for the people who can actually use it.

The build

What shipped

Process explainer

Three steps, zero office visits, so the whole transaction is legible in ten seconds.

Eight document types

Rent agreement, affidavit, power of attorney, sale deed, business contract, partnership deed, undertaking, indemnity bond — each named, because each is a separate search.

Flat pricing block

One fee, what it includes, and what is charged separately at official rates.

WhatsApp order builder

Pre-writes the order message from three fields. Sends nothing without the visitor pressing send.

Seven-question FAQ

Legality, what you need to provide, what the fee covers, overseas use, turnaround and more.

Lead capture to Google Sheets

Orders logged to a sheet the client actually opens, instead of an inbox that fills up.

Meta Pixel

So the client can retarget and measure ad spend against real orders.

Overseas Pakistani path

Power of attorney for overseas clients is called out specifically — a high-value segment that cannot visit an office by definition.

What we took from it

What this project taught us

Most service businesses in Pakistan already run on WhatsApp and then bolt a website on the side that routes people into a contact form nobody checks. It is backwards. If the deal closes in a chat, the site's only job is to get someone into that chat already convinced, with their question half-answered and the price already known.

The other lesson is that publishing the price is a competitive weapon in a market where nobody does. Al-Qanoon's flat PKR 1,000 fee is the single most persuasive thing on the page, precisely because the alternative is an agent who will not tell you.
